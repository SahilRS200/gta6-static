Coming from GTA 5 and wondering why holding the aim button no longer snaps you onto the nearest enemy? That's deliberate. GTA 6 doesn't start you on lock-on, but you can switch it back on in a few seconds. **No story spoilers.**

## Why lock-on isn't the default

Rockstar's VP of Technology, **David Hynd**, told Final Weapon that the team decided early on to **remove lock-on as the default targeting mode**. Instead they built up free aim and aim assist so the shooting holds up on its own ([Final Weapon](https://finalweapon.net/2026/11/25/gta-6-interview-rockstar-games-world-design-music-combat/)). Lock-on is still in the game; it's just not the starting setting.

## How to turn on lock-on

1. **Pause** the game and open **Settings**.
2. Go to the **Gameplay** tab.
3. Under **Combat**, find **Targeting**. It has three options: **Partial** (the default), **Free Aim** and **Lock-On** ([BGR](https://www.bgr.com/2321914/how-to-adjust-aim-assist-gta-6-guide/)).
4. Pick **Lock-On**. Now aiming snaps to the nearest target, like GTA 5. You can switch modes any time, even mid-mission.

## Which one to pick

- **Lock-On:** the most help. Good for car chases, drive-bys and if you just want the story. Game Rant's relaxed setup pairs it with Aim Assist Strength **3** and Lock-On Time **5** ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/gta-6-best-aim-settings-casual-realistic-gameplay/)).
- **Partial:** pulls your aim toward a nearby target without locking on. A middle ground.
- **Free Aim:** full control, no snapping. Game Rant's hardcore setup turns aim assist to **0**.
